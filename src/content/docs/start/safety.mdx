Six things. Read them before your first transaction; the linked pages have the
detail.

## 1. Your keys stay in your wallet

Core is non-custodial. It never holds your keys and never moves an asset
without a signature you approve in your own wallet.

**Nobody working on Bitcoin Universe will ever ask for your seed phrase or
private key.** There is no support process that needs either. Treat any such
request as an attack, whoever it appears to come from.

## 2. Confirm the address before connecting

Core is at `www.bitcoinuniverse.io`. The bare `bitcoinuniverse.io` redirects to
a different application.

## 3. Unavailable is not empty

A market that says its reader is unavailable is telling you the read failed,
not that the market is empty. A figure shown as a dash was not measured; it is
not a zero.

Do not create or repeat a transaction from stale market data.
[Reading the numbers](/docs-core/start/reading-the-numbers/).

## 4. An unavailable action is a safety decision

An action Core will not perform is not proof that an asset or market does not
exist. Nine of the 29 marketplace protocols support neither buying nor
listing, thirteen sit behind a feature gate, and any protocol's live authority
can close at any time. The [support matrix](/docs-core/protocols/support-matrix/) says which,
and why.

Core's checks fail closed: a required mismatch **blocks** an action rather than
downgrading to a warning you can click past.

## 5. An asset can live on a single satoshi

An output holding an inscription looks exactly like ordinary change. Spend it
as change or use it as fee padding and the asset is gone, in a transaction
Bitcoin considers perfectly valid.

Worse, a malformed rune marker destroys every rune balance a transaction spent,
and no validity check can see it. Core reads that marker and refuses.
[Asset-bearing outputs](/docs-core/explorer/asset-bearing-outputs/).

## 6. Read the wallet, not just the screen

Core's review shows the asset, the amount, the service fee, the network fee
estimate, and the total before your wallet asks you to sign. That is a summary
of what Core intends.

**Your wallet shows the transaction's own inputs and outputs. Read them
there.** The wallet is the final authorization boundary.

A broadcast transaction cannot be recalled.
