The USD figures beside Bitcoin amounts, in portfolio totals, floor prices, fee
costs, and the header price chip, come from one Universe-operated market price
service that the browser reads once a minute. **No exchange API is called from
the page.**

## What happens when there is no quote

- **Before the service has answered, and when it cannot answer at all, there is
  no price.** The USD figure is left out. It is never replaced with a
  placeholder number, a last-known guess with no age, or `$0`.
- **The Bitcoin amount is always shown.** Fiat is a convenience on top of it,
  so losing the quote never hides the number that matters.
- **A quote that did answer is kept in the browser for up to a day**, so a
  single failed refresh still shows the last measured price rather than
  nothing.

## The price says where it came from and when

Open the fee panel in the header. The line under the price reads the source and
the time it was measured, for example "Coinbase, 16:05".

A quote resting on yesterday's reading shows yesterday's time. **A stale price
is visible rather than silent**, which is the whole point: a number with no
timestamp is indistinguishable from a fresh one, and that is exactly the
confusion this product refuses to create.

## Why this one figure is different

Core reads chain data from its own infrastructure and says so. The exchange
rate is the one figure it does not, because Bitcoin Universe does not run an
exchange.

It is read once a minute by the API and shared, so no visitor's browser
contacts the exchange directly. Chain data is never sourced this way.

## What to do with it

Treat the fiat figure as a convenience for orientation. Every decision that
matters, a price, a fee, a total, is denominated in satoshis, and that is what
your wallet will sign.
